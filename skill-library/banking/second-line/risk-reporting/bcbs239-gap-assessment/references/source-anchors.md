# Source anchors: bcbs239-gap-assessment

Cited regulatory and supervisory sources this skill anchors to. Each primary anchor names the source, the section reference (or `[verify section]` flag), a one-paragraph excerpt or paraphrase of the relevant content, and the link.

The skill cites these by file path. SKILL.md does not restate excerpts.

## Primary anchors

### 1. BCBS 239 — Principles for Effective Risk Data Aggregation and Risk Reporting

- Citation: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, "Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting" (BCBS 239), January 2013. Fourteen principles arranged in four groups.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: BCBS 239 sets supervisory expectations for the risk data aggregation and risk reporting capabilities of banks designated as global systemically important (G-SIBs), with national supervisors expected to apply the principles to domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs) within three years of designation. The fourteen principles are the structural backbone of any conforming gap assessment:
  - Group A — Overarching governance and infrastructure: Principle 1 (Governance), Principle 2 (Data architecture and IT infrastructure).
  - Group B — Risk data aggregation capabilities: Principle 3 (Accuracy and integrity), Principle 4 (Completeness), Principle 5 (Timeliness), Principle 6 (Adaptability).
  - Group C — Risk reporting practices: Principle 7 (Accuracy), Principle 8 (Comprehensiveness), Principle 9 (Clarity and usefulness), Principle 10 (Frequency), Principle 11 (Distribution).
  - Group D — Supervisory review, tools, and cooperation: Principle 12 (Review), Principle 13 (Remedial actions and supervisory measures), Principle 14 (Home/host cooperation).
- Link: https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs239.htm (Principles 1-14, four-group structure)
- Notes: The four-group structure and the rating vocabulary (compliant, largely compliant, materially non-compliant, non-compliant) used in BCBS thematic reviews are the structural inheritance of this skill. Principle 12 is supervisor-facing and read at the firm as a readiness expectation. Principles 13 and 14 read at the firm as posture toward open findings and toward home/host coordination; they are not self-rated in the same way as Principles 1 to 11.

### 2. BCBS Progress Reports and Newsletters on the implementation of the principles

- Citation: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, progress reports and newsletters on the adoption of the principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting. Published reports include December 2013, January 2015, December 2015, March 2017, June 2018, April 2020, and November 2023, plus the BCBS Newsletter on RDARR implementation issued January 2026 [verify exact title and publication date].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The progress-report series rates G-SIB compliance principle by principle. Persistent thematic findings concentrate in Principle 2 (data architecture and IT infrastructure), Principle 3 (accuracy and integrity, particularly dependence on manual workarounds), and Principle 6 (adaptability — ad-hoc and stress-scenario aggregation within required timeframes). The 2023 report restated that no G-SIB had achieved full compliance with all principles in the decade since BCBS 239 issuance. The January 2026 Newsletter is the most recent supervisory commentary on persistent gaps and on board-level data-quality framework expectations; it is supervisory commentary, not new rule text.
- Link: https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publications.htm — filter on "risk data aggregation"; the Progress Reports series and the January 2026 Newsletter on RDARR
- Notes: Use the most recent progress report or newsletter as the comparator for direction-of-travel commentary in the executive summary. The thematic-finding pattern is what supervisors expect a self-assessment to address head-on; an assessment that rates Principle 6 as `largely-compliant` without recent ad-hoc-aggregation evidence is not credible against the BCBS pattern.

### 3. ECB Guide on effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting

- Citation: European Central Bank, "Guide on effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting", May 2024 (final version following 2023 consultation) [verify current version].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The ECB Guide consolidates SSM supervisory expectations on RDARR for significant institutions, building on BCBS 239 and on findings from the SSM's RDARR thematic review. The Guide adds incremental expectations beyond BCBS 239 baseline, including: explicit board-level responsibilities for the data-quality framework; a formal data-management committee with named composition and cadence; documented data-lineage covering source-to-report paths for material risk types; an integrated reporting taxonomy across financial, regulatory, statistical, and risk reporting; and quantitative data-quality metrics with breach-driven escalation. The Guide is the operative document for SSM-supervised firms and for the EU subsidiaries of cross-border G-SIBs.
- Link: https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/ecb/pub/pdf/ssm.RDARRguide202405~bbab9b8c25.en.pdf — ECB Guide May 2024 [verify section]
- Notes: For an ECB-supervised entity or for a G-SIB with material EU operations, surface ECB Guide expectations as supplementary criteria within Principles 1, 2, 3, 6, and 11. Distinguish BCBS 239 baseline from ECB Guide overlay so the assessment reads from either supervisor's lens.

### 4. FRB SR 12-17 — Consolidated Supervision Framework for Large Financial Institutions

- Citation: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, Supervision and Regulation Letter SR 12-17, "Consolidated Supervision Framework for Large Financial Institutions" (December 17, 2012), section on management of core business lines and risks, including risk-data and risk-reporting capabilities. Operationalised for LFI ratings by SR 19-3 (LFI rating system).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: SR 12-17 sets the consolidated supervisory framework for LFIs and articulates the expectation that LFI management has the risk-data infrastructure to aggregate exposures and concentrations across the firm, support stress testing, and produce timely and accurate risk reporting to the board and senior management. The letter is the US supervisory expectation chain that BCBS 239 capabilities are present at large BHCs; the LFI rating components in SR 19-3 are where risk-data weaknesses surface in supervisory ratings.
- Link: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1217.htm — SR 12-17; https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1903.htm — SR 19-3 LFI rating system
- Notes: SR 12-17 predates BCBS 239 final issuance by one month and was the FRB's framing for what BCBS 239 codified. For a US LFI, the SR 12-17 frame plus the SR 19-3 rating system is the supervisory expectation chain; the BCBS 239 principles are the assessment grammar. The FRB has periodically issued horizontal-review themes on RDARR through supervisory letters and presentations rather than a single named "BCBS 239 SR letter"; cite the operative letter or theme at write time and do not cite SR 21-19 (which has been removed).

### 5. OCC Heightened Standards (12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D)

- Citation: 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D, "OCC Guidelines Establishing Heightened Standards for Certain Large Insured National Banks, Insured Federal Savings Associations, and Insured Federal Branches", §II "Risk Governance Framework" [verify section labels for risk-data infrastructure expectations].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The Heightened Standards apply to OCC-supervised banks meeting the asset-size threshold (currently $50B). §II sets the risk-governance framework, including independent risk management, board composition and oversight, risk-appetite statements, and supporting infrastructure. Risk-data infrastructure that supports the risk-appetite statement and the risk-reporting that flows to the board sits inside the §II expectations and connects to BCBS 239 Principles 1, 2, and 11.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-30 — 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D
- Notes: For OCC-supervised covered banks, the Heightened Standards are the prudential reach of risk-data expectations beyond BCBS 239 baseline. Cite alongside SR 12-17 in the supervisory-context section of the assessment.

## Secondary anchors (overlay only)

### 6. Basel III monitoring exercise data templates

- Citation: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, Basel III monitoring exercise instructions and reporting templates [verify current version].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The semi-annual Basel III monitoring exercise collects standardised data from participating banks. The aggregation discipline required to populate the templates is a working test of risk-data-aggregation capability and an evidentiary thread for Principle 4 (completeness) and Principle 6 (adaptability).
- Link: https://www.bis.org/bcbs/qis/ (Basel III monitoring exercise instructions, section on reporting templates) [verify section]
- Notes: Use as a working evidence pointer, not as a primary assessment anchor.

### 7. COSO ERM 2017 — Enterprise Risk Management Integrating with Strategy and Performance

- Citation: Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission, "Enterprise Risk Management Integrating with Strategy and Performance" (June 2017).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: COSO ERM 2017 frames the broader enterprise risk management model in which the firm's risk data architecture sits. Principles around governance, information, communication, and reporting connect to BCBS 239 Principles 1, 2, 9, and 10 at the ERM level.
- Link: https://www.coso.org/erm (COSO ERM 2017, principle 19 on information and communication; principle 20 on reporting on risk, culture, and performance)
- Notes: Use as a framing anchor for the executive-summary commentary where the assessment connects BCBS 239 posture to the wider ERM model. Not a substitute for BCBS 239 itself.

### 8. NAIC ORSA Model Act (Model #505)

- Citation: NAIC Model #505, "Risk Management and Own Risk and Solvency Assessment Model Act" (current version), ORSA Summary Report sections on risk identification, measurement, monitoring, and reporting [verify section].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: ORSA requires insurers above the premium threshold to maintain risk-management functions including risk identification, measurement, monitoring, and reporting. The data-aggregation and reporting expectations under ORSA carry analogues to BCBS 239 Principles 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, and 10, and are the relevant frame where an insurance group voluntarily applies BCBS 239 as its internal benchmark.
- Link: https://content.naic.org/cipr-topics/own-risk-and-solvency-assessment-orsa (NAIC Model #505, ORSA Summary Report section on risk identification, measurement, monitoring, and reporting) [verify section]
- Notes: Insurance overlay anchor. BCBS 239 does not directly apply to insurers; this anchor frames the analogous expectations.

### 9. IAIS Insurance Core Principles — ICP 8 and ICP 16

- Citation: International Association of Insurance Supervisors, Insurance Core Principles, ICP 8 (Risk Management and Internal Controls) and ICP 16 (Enterprise Risk Management for Solvency Purposes) [verify section].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: ICP 8 and ICP 16 set IAIS expectations on the insurer's risk-management framework, internal controls, and ERM for solvency purposes. The data-aggregation and reporting expectations carry analogues to BCBS 239 Principles 1, 3, 4, 7, 8, and 10.
- Link: https://www.iaisweb.org/icps-and-comframe/ (ICP 8 section on risk management and internal controls; ICP 16 section on ERM for solvency purposes) [verify section]
- Notes: Insurance overlay anchor. Used where an insurance group operating across jurisdictions adopts BCBS 239 as a maturity benchmark for the risk-data layer.

### 10. SEC Rule 17h — Risk assessment recordkeeping for associated persons of broker-dealers

- Citation: 17 CFR §240.17h-1T and §240.17h-2T (Risk assessment recordkeeping and reporting requirements for brokers and dealers).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Rule 17h requires certain broker-dealers to maintain and report risk-assessment information about material associated persons. The risk-data layer that supports 17h reporting overlaps with the BHC-level risk-data layer that supports BCBS 239 at the parent level; the broker-dealer subsidiary inherits the parent's BCBS 239 posture through the consolidated group.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/section-240.17h-1T (17 CFR §240.17h-1T and §240.17h-2T)
- Notes: Capital-markets overlay anchor. Used for the broker-dealer subsidiary view in a BHC-level BCBS 239 assessment.
