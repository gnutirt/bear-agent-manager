# Source anchors: risk-committee-pack

Cited regulatory sources this skill anchors to. Every primary anchor names the source, the section reference (or `[verify section]` flag), a one-paragraph paraphrase of what the source says that the skill relies on, and the link.

The skill cites these by file path. SKILL.md does not restate excerpts.

## Primary anchors

### 1. BCBS 239 — Principles for Effective Risk Data Aggregation and Risk Reporting

- Citation: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, "Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting" (January 2013), Principles 7-11 (risk reporting practices). Supervisory context: the BCBS January 2026 Newsletter on the implementation of risk data aggregation and risk reporting [verify exact title and publication date] continues the BCBS-progress-report tradition of flagging persistent thematic findings on Principles 2, 3, and 6, and on the data-quality framework at the management-body level.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Principle 7 (accuracy) requires risk reports to reconcile and validate to source data. Principle 8 (comprehensiveness) requires reports to cover all material risk areas with appropriate aggregation. Principle 9 (clarity and usefulness) requires reports tailored to recipient needs in clear, concise terms. Principle 10 (frequency) matches reporting frequency to the recipient's decision-making cadence and the volatility of the risk. Principle 11 (distribution) sets the confidentiality and need-to-know controls. The committee pack consumes Principles 7-11 for the data-confidence label and the BCBS 239 known-limitations appendix; Principles 1-6 (governance and data-aggregation infrastructure) sit upstream and are addressed by `bcbs239-gap-assessment`.
- Link: https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs239.htm — Principles 7-11; https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publications.htm — BCBS Newsletter on RDARR (January 2026 issue) [verify exact URL]
- Notes: BCBS 239 is supervisory guidance; for US G-SIBs and other in-scope firms, prudential regulators incorporate it through the supervisory cycle. For non-G-SIBs, the principles are leading practice and frequently appear in OCC Heightened Standards readiness reviews. The 2026 Newsletter is supervisory commentary, not a new rule; cite for supervisory context, not as a binding source.

### 2. COSO ERM 2017 — Enterprise Risk Management: Integrating with Strategy and Performance

- Citation: Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission, "Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance" (June 2017), Components: Governance and Culture; Strategy and Objective-Setting; Performance; Review and Revision; Information, Communication, and Reporting.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The 2017 framework reframes ERM from a risk-list orientation to a strategy-and-performance orientation. The Information, Communication, and Reporting component sets the expectation that risk information flows internally and externally with sufficient quality and frequency to support decision-making. The Governance and Culture component sets the board's ERM oversight role; the Performance component covers risk identification, severity assessment, prioritization, and response. The committee pack architecture (heat map, top risks, performance against risk appetite, escalation) maps to these components.
- Link: https://www.coso.org/enterprise-risk-management — ERM 2017 framework, Information Communication and Reporting component (Principle 18)
- Notes: COSO ERM is voluntary framework guidance; heavily adopted across US financial-services firms as the enterprise framing. Distinct from COSO Internal Control 2013, which sits at the control level.

### 3. OCC Heightened Standards — 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D

- Citation: 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D, "OCC Guidelines Establishing Heightened Standards for Certain Large Insured National Banks, Insured Federal Savings Associations, and Insured Federal Branches". The published Appendix uses lettered Roman-numeral headings (I Introduction; II Risk Governance Framework, including the eight risk categories; III Risk Appetite Statement; IV Roles and Responsibilities, covering front-line units, independent risk management, and internal audit; V Strategic Plan; VI Talent Management; etc.) [verify section labels at pack-write time — the Appendix uses letters without the III.B-style sub-numbering colloquially used].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Section II requires covered banks to establish and adhere to a Risk Governance Framework covering eight risk categories (credit, interest rate, liquidity, price, operational, compliance, strategic, and reputation). Section III requires a written Risk Appetite Statement with both qualitative components and quantitative limits, and provides for monitoring and reporting against those limits. Section IV requires the board (or an independent risk committee) to review and approve the framework and oversee compliance. The committee pack is the principal governance instrument that demonstrates board oversight under Section IV and surfaces RAS performance under Section III.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-30/appendix-Appendix%20D%20to%20Part%2030 — 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D
- Notes: Heightened Standards apply to OCC-supervised insured national banks, federal savings associations, and federal branches with average total consolidated assets at or above the threshold (currently $50 billion). For smaller covered banks, OCC may apply by reservation of authority. Section labels should be confirmed against the current eCFR text at pack-write time.

### 4. FRB SR 12-17 — Consolidated Supervision Framework for Large Financial Institutions

- Citation: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, Supervision and Regulation Letter SR 12-17 / CA 12-14, "Consolidated Supervision Framework for Large Financial Institutions" (December 17, 2012).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: SR 12-17 sets the FRB's consolidated supervisory expectations for large financial institutions, organized around two primary objectives: enhancing resiliency (capital, liquidity, corporate governance, recovery planning) and reducing the impact of failure (resolution planning, additional capital and liquidity buffers, more intensive supervisory oversight). Risk-committee reporting at a BHC level should reference the consolidated framework and the rated components, including the LFI rating system that succeeded the historical RFI/C(D) rating for LFIs.
- Link: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1217.htm — SR 12-17 Consolidated Supervision Framework
- Notes: SR 12-17 applies to large financial institutions including BHCs and SLHCs above defined thresholds and certain foreign banking organizations. The LFI rating system framework was published as SR 19-3 (Large Financial Institution Rating System) and operationalizes SR 12-17 ratings; the committee pack typically references both for BHC-level reporting.

### 5. Dodd-Frank Section 165(b) — Risk Committee Requirements (Reg YY, 12 CFR Part 252)

- Citation: Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act §165(b)(1)(B)(iv), codified at 12 USC §5365(b), implemented by Federal Reserve Regulation YY at 12 CFR Part 252. Risk-committee requirements run through Subpart C (US BHCs above the threshold; §252.22 covers covered company risk-committee composition and risk-management expectations), with Subpart D parallel provisions for foreign banking organisations and Subpart B / §252.5 setting the tailoring categories that scope which enhanced prudential standards apply [verify current section labels and tailoring-category applicability post-EGRRCPA].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Section 165(b) requires the Federal Reserve to issue prudential standards for large BHCs, including a requirement that publicly traded BHCs above the threshold establish a board-level risk committee responsible for overseeing enterprise-wide risk-management practices. Reg YY operationalises that obligation by setting committee charter, composition, independence, and risk-management-expertise requirements; the tailoring-category framework at §252.5 determines which Reg YY enhanced standards apply at which BHC tier.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-252 — 12 CFR Part 252 (Reg YY), Subparts B / C / D
- Notes: EGRRCPA (2018) raised the §165 threshold for many enhanced prudential standards from $50 billion to $250 billion, with FRB discretion in the $100-250 billion range. The risk-committee requirement under §165(b)(1)(B)(iv) sits inside the broader Reg YY frame; verify the current threshold and applicability against the §252.5 categories at pack-write time. For BHCs in the lowest publicly traded threshold tier, an FRB-supervised risk-management committee at the consolidated level has been a long-running supervisory expectation through Reg YY rather than a single fixed-asset trigger.

### 6. NAIC ORSA Guidance Manual — Risk and Solvency Assessment

- Citation: National Association of Insurance Commissioners, Risk Management and Own Risk and Solvency Assessment Model Act (#505) and ORSA Guidance Manual (current edition), Sections 1 (Description of the Insurer's Risk Management Framework), 2 (Insurer's Assessment of Risk Exposure), 3 (Group Risk Capital and Prospective Solvency Assessment).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Model #505 requires insurers above the premium threshold to maintain a risk-management framework and to file an annual ORSA Summary Report with their lead state regulator. The Summary Report covers the framework, the insurer's assessment of risk exposure under both normal and stressed conditions, and the group risk-capital and prospective solvency assessment. Carriers frequently use the enterprise risk committee pack as the principal supporting document for the framework and exposure-assessment sections; the insurance overlay maps committee-pack sections to ORSA Summary Report sections.
- Link: https://content.naic.org/cipr-topics/own-risk-and-solvency-assessment-orsa — Model #505 §4 and ORSA Guidance Manual Sections 1, 2, 3
- Notes: Model #505 is binding only to the extent state DOIs adopt it; adoption status is tracked by NAIC. Premium threshold for filing is $500 million for a single insurer or $1 billion for an insurance group, as adopted by most states. Lead state framework determines the receiving regulator.

### 7. FCA SYSC and Risk Function Guidelines — UK risk-function expectations

- Citation: Financial Conduct Authority Handbook, Senior Management Arrangements, Systems and Controls (SYSC), in particular SYSC 4.1 (General organisational requirements), SYSC 7.1 (Risk control), SYSC 21 (Risk control: additional guidance), and (for in-scope firms) SYSC 4.3A (Management body of dual-regulated firms) [verify current section numbering at pack-write time].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: SYSC 7.1 requires firms to establish, implement and maintain risk-management policies and procedures and to monitor the adequacy and effectiveness of those arrangements. SYSC 21 sets additional guidance on the risk-control function for IFPRU firms (and successor regimes), including the role of the chief risk officer. For dual-regulated firms, SYSC 4.3A sets the management-body governance frame including risk-committee composition. The committee pack supports the SYSC obligations for in-scope UK-regulated entities.
- Link: https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/SYSC/ — SYSC Section 7.1 Risk control; SYSC Section 21 (where applicable)
- Notes: SYSC reach varies by firm category and dual-regulation status (FCA vs PRA). For PRA-regulated banks and PRA-designated investment firms, the PRA Rulebook General Organisational Requirements and Risk Control parts apply alongside SYSC; the committee pack should reference both where applicable. Verify current section numbering against the FCA Handbook reader at pack-write time as SYSC has been restructured multiple times.

## Cross-cutting overlays cited

### 8. SEC Climate Disclosure Rule (2024, non-operative) and TCFD / ISSB IFRS S2 (operative voluntary frame)

- Citation: SEC Release Nos. 33-11275, 34-99678, "The Enhancement and Standardization of Climate-Related Disclosures for Investors" (final March 6, 2024), Items 1500-1507 of Regulation S-K and Article 14 of Regulation S-X. TCFD: "Recommendations of the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures" (Final Report, June 2017) and "Implementing the Recommendations of the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures" (October 2021). ISSB IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures (June 2023), incorporating TCFD.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The SEC final rule (had it taken effect) would have required registrants to disclose material climate-related risks, governance, risk-management, targets, and (for larger registrants) Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions, with certain financial-statement disclosures on severe-weather effects. TCFD organises climate-related financial disclosure into four pillars and is the dominant voluntary frame; ISSB IFRS S2 carries the same architecture into the IFRS sustainability standard set. Most firms reporting climate to risk committees use TCFD or IFRS S2 as the substantive content frame.
- Link: https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/33-11275.pdf — SEC Release Nos. 33-11275, 34-99678; https://www.fsb-tcfd.org/recommendations/ — TCFD Recommendations; https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/ifrs-sustainability-standards-navigator/ifrs-s2-climate-related-disclosures/ — ISSB IFRS S2
- Notes: The SEC climate rule is treated as non-operative pending verification at pack date. The Commission stayed the rule (Order April 4, 2024) and SEC posture has shifted; do not assert current effectiveness without re-verifying. Default posture: lean on TCFD / IFRS S2 for substantive content and cite the SEC rule only with an explicit current-state check at pack-write time. The FSB disbanded the TCFD in October 2023 with monitoring transferred to the IFRS Foundation; TCFD recommendations remain the working frame and underpin IFRS S2.

### 9. NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 and Generative AI Profile

- Citation: National Institute of Standards and Technology, AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (January 2023), Govern function (Govern 1.x board oversight). NIST AI 600-1, "Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile" (July 2024) for AI committee-pack content where relevant.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: NIST AI RMF Govern function covers organizational policies, procedures, and risk-tolerance setting for AI risk including board-level oversight responsibilities. AI risk content carried into the enterprise risk committee pack (rather than a dedicated AI committee pack) maps to the Govern function and to the firm's tier-1 model inventory.
- Link: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework — AI RMF 1.0 Govern function (Section Govern 1.x) and AI 600-1 GenAI Profile [verify section]
- Notes: NIST AI RMF is voluntary. The full AI committee pack lives in `ai-governance-model-risk/skills/board-ai-risk-pack`; this skill carries the enterprise-level AI risk slice (typically a brief in the heat map and top-risks sections, with deeper material in the AI committee pack).

### 10. NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 — board reporting on cybersecurity

- Citation: New York Department of Financial Services, 23 NYCRR Part 500, "Cybersecurity Requirements for Financial Services Companies", §500.4(b) (CISO report to board) and §500.4(d) (board oversight) (current text reflecting Second Amendment effective November 1, 2023, with phased compliance dates).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §500.4(b) requires the CISO to report in writing at least annually to the senior governing body on the covered entity's cybersecurity program, including material cybersecurity issues, plans for remediation, and assessment of confidentiality, integrity and security of nonpublic information. §500.4(d) requires the senior governing body to exercise oversight including approval of the cybersecurity policy. The committee pack carries the enterprise-level cyber slice (heat map, top risks, material events); deeper cyber-disclosure material lives in `cyber-disclosure-readiness`.
- Link: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/cybersecurity — 23 NYCRR Part 500 §500.4
- Notes: Part 500 reach is to NYDFS-licensed covered entities; many cross-state and multinational firms are in scope through a NY licence even where their primary regulator is elsewhere.
