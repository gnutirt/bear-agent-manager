# Source anchors: management-response

Cited regulatory sources this skill anchors to. Every primary anchor names the source, the section reference (or `[verify section]` flag), a one-paragraph paraphrase of what the source says that the skill relies on, and the link.

The skill cites these by file path. SKILL.md does not restate excerpts.

## Primary anchors

### 1. FRB SR 13-13 — Supervisory Considerations for the Communication of Supervisory Findings

- Citation: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, Supervision and Regulation Letter SR 13-13, "Supervisory Considerations for the Communication of Supervisory Findings" (June 17, 2013), with attached supervisory guidance.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: SR 13-13 sets the FRB's framework for communicating supervisory findings, distinguishing Matters Requiring Immediate Attention (MRIAs) from Matters Requiring Attention (MRAs). MRIAs require immediate attention and remediation; MRAs require timely remediation but on a less compressed timeline. The letter sets the supervisory expectation that the firm respond with a written corrective-action plan including ownership, milestones, and a target date for completion, and that progress against the plan be reported on a cadence the supervisor specifies. SR 13-13 is the principal source for the MRA / MRIA convention and the management-response posture the FRB expects.
- Link: https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1313.htm — SR 13-13, paragraphs on MRA / MRIA distinction and management-response framework
- Notes: SR 13-13 applies to FRB-supervised institutions including BHCs, SLHCs, state member banks, and U.S. operations of foreign banking organizations. The framework is incorporated into the FRB's Commercial Bank Examination Manual and the Bank Holding Company Supervision Manual. Verify the current text against the FRB letter index at write time; SR 13-13 is stable but accompanying guidance evolves.

### 2. OCC findings-communication framework (Comptroller's Handbook Bank Supervision Process; PPM 5310-3 enforcement actions)

- Citation: Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, Comptroller's Handbook, "Bank Supervision Process" booklet (current edition) [verify edition], chapters on Findings Communication and Corrective Actions — the operative source for OCC MRA / MRIA convention and management-response expectation. PPM 5310-3 is the OCC's policy directive on bank enforcement actions (including formal and informal enforcement orders), separate from the routine findings-communication framework; cite PPM 5310-3 only where the matter has escalated to enforcement.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The Bank Supervision Process booklet sets the OCC's findings-communication convention (Matters Requiring Attention) and the expected management-response form on routine supervisory findings. PPM 5310-3 (enforcement actions) is the OCC's directive on the enforcement process — formal agreements, consent orders, civil money penalties, removal and prohibition, and related actions — and applies once a matter has escalated beyond routine supervisory communication. The management-response artifact for a routine MRA cites the Bank Supervision Process booklet; the response artifact for a matter inside an enforcement action cites the operative enforcement order and routes through outside counsel.
- Link: https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/bank-supervision-process/pub-ch-bank-supervision-process.pdf — Bank Supervision Process booklet; https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/index-comptrollers-handbook.html — Comptroller's Handbook index
- Notes: Earlier drafts of this skill mis-cited PPM 5310-3 as the routine findings-communication directive; the operative source for routine findings is the Bank Supervision Process booklet, and PPM 5310-3 is reserved for the enforcement-action lane. PPM numbers are revised periodically; verify the current PPM number and revision at write time.

### 3. OCC Comptroller's Handbook — Bank Supervision Process

- Citation: Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, Comptroller's Handbook, "Bank Supervision Process" booklet (current version; verify revision date at write time), chapters on Findings Communication and Corrective Actions [verify section labels].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The Bank Supervision Process booklet sets the OCC's overall supervisory framework, including the supervisory cycle, examination scoping, findings communication, and corrective-action follow-up. The findings-communication chapter sets the convention for Matters Requiring Attention and the expected management-response form. The corrective-actions chapter sets the supervisory follow-up cadence, including the OCC's expectation that management report progress against committed milestones.
- Link: https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/files/bank-supervision-process/pub-ch-bank-supervision-process.pdf — Bank Supervision Process booklet, Findings Communication and Corrective Actions sections [verify section labels]
- Notes: The Comptroller's Handbook is structured as a set of booklets; verify booklet revision date and section labels against the current OCC publications page at write time. The booklet is supervisory guidance, not binding regulation; binding obligations sit in the underlying statute, regulation, and order.

### 4. FDIC Risk Management Manual of Examination Policies

- Citation: Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, Risk Management Manual of Examination Policies, sections on Examination Findings and Corrective Actions [verify section labels at write time].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The FDIC Risk Management Manual sets the FDIC's framework for examining state non-member banks and other FDIC-supervised institutions. The findings-and-corrective-actions sections describe the FDIC's supervisory finding conventions (Matters Requiring Board Attention as the FDIC equivalent of the FRB's MRA in many examination contexts) and the expected management-response form. The examiner-in-charge communicates findings in the report of examination; the institution's response is appended to the report and tracked for resolution.
- Link: https://www.fdic.gov/regulations/safety/manual/ — FDIC Risk Management Manual of Examination Policies, Examination Findings and Corrective Actions sections [verify section labels]
- Notes: FDIC examination conventions vary somewhat by examination type (safety-and-soundness, BSA/AML, IT, trust). Verify the section labels and the current finding-communication conventions against the manual at write time. FDIC also issues Financial Institution Letters (FILs) on supervisory topics; FIL series numbers and dates should be cited where a particular FIL informs the response.

### 5. NYDFS supervisory finding communications

- Citation: New York Department of Financial Services, supervisory communications (no single named source); examination conventions are set out in industry letters, examination protocols under 23 NYCRR Part 500 §500.17(b) and related procedures, and direct supervisory correspondence to NYDFS-licensed entities.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: NYDFS conducts examinations under 23 NYCRR and communicates findings through the report of examination and supervisory correspondence. Findings range from informal observations to formal Matters Requiring Attention-equivalent supervisory letters. Part 500 examinations (cybersecurity) are the most documented case in published industry letters; the framework is otherwise supervisory practice rather than a single named source. Management response follows the convention the NYDFS examination team specifies in the communication.
- Link: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/industry-letters — NYDFS Industry Letters index; 23 NYCRR Part 500 §500.17(b) (annual CISO certification, Form B) for the cybersecurity examination case
- Notes: NYDFS supervisory finding communication is not codified in a single named source; this anchor is framed as supervisory practice rather than a binding rule. For the cybersecurity case, Part 500 §500.17(b) and related amendments (effective November 1, 2023, with phased compliance through November 2025) set the formal framework. Verify section labels post-amendment at write time.

### 6. IIA Global Internal Audit Standards (effective January 9, 2025)

- Citation: Institute of Internal Auditors, "Global Internal Audit Standards" (effective January 9, 2025), with the relevant standards on communicating engagement results and the chief audit executive's follow-up process [verify exact standard numbers under the new framework — the standards have been re-architected from the 2017 numbering and the equivalents to 2410 (Criteria for Communicating), 2421 (Errors and Omissions), and 2500.A1 (CAE follow-up) sit under the new Domain / Principle / Standard structure]. The 2025 Global Standards replaced the prior International Standards for the Professional Practice of Internal Auditing in their entirety.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The Global Standards retain the substantive expectations on engagement communication (criteria, condition, cause, effect, recommendation; management response convention) and on chief audit executive follow-up to confirm that management actions are effectively implemented or that senior management has accepted the residual risk. The management-response artifact carries the action commitments the CAE follow-up tracks; the evidence-of-effectiveness plan is what the CAE inspects at follow-up.
- Link: https://www.theiia.org/en/standards/2024-standards/ — IIA Global Internal Audit Standards (effective January 9, 2025)
- Notes: Engagements chartered before January 9, 2025 may still operate under the 2017 Standards in a transition mode; verify which framework the audit function is operating under at write time. Substantive expectations on management response and CAE follow-up persist across both versions; standard numbering does not, so cite by the operative framework's structure.

### 7. PCAOB AS 2201 — Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting

- Citation: Public Company Accounting Oversight Board, Auditing Standard 2201, "An Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting That Is Integrated with An Audit of Financial Statements", paragraphs on identified deficiencies and material weaknesses, including paragraphs on management's response to identified deficiencies [verify pin cites at write time].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: AS 2201 sets the auditor's responsibilities when conducting an integrated audit of internal control over financial reporting. The standard distinguishes control deficiencies, significant deficiencies, and material weaknesses; for material weaknesses identified during the audit, management must report them in the annual report and the auditor reports them in the auditor's report. Management's response to identified deficiencies (including remediation plans) is consequential to whether the deficiency is appropriately classified and to the audit-committee's oversight of remediation. The management-response artifact for an external-audit ICFR finding follows the AS 2201 framework.
- Link: https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201 — PCAOB AS 2201, paragraphs on material weakness, significant deficiency, and management's response [verify pin cites]
- Notes: PCAOB standards apply to audits of issuers (public companies) and broker-dealers. AS 2201 paragraphs are referenced by the prior AS 5 numbering in some legacy materials; verify the current numbering against the PCAOB standards page at write time. The skill's external-audit response template follows AS 2201 conventions where the finding affects ICFR.

## Secondary anchors

### 8. COSO ERM 2017 — Review and Revision component

- Citation: Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission, "Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance" (June 2017), Review and Revision component (Principles 17-20).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The Review and Revision component of the 2017 ERM framework sets the expectation that the firm reviews ERM practices and revises them as substantial change occurs. The management response to a finding is one of the principal triggers for review and revision: the response commits the firm to changes in process, control, or governance, and the post-remediation evidence-of-effectiveness check is the loop that closes the review-and-revision cycle for that finding. The skill's evidence-of-effectiveness plan section maps to this component.
- Link: https://www.coso.org/enterprise-risk-management — ERM 2017 framework, Review and Revision component (Principles 17-20)
- Notes: COSO ERM is voluntary framework guidance; widely adopted across US financial-services firms as the enterprise framing. The mapping between management-response sections and the Review and Revision principles is a leading-practice articulation, not a binding requirement.

### 9. Three Lines Model (IIA 2020 revision)

- Citation: Institute of Internal Auditors, "The IIA's Three Lines Model: An update of the Three Lines of Defense" (July 2020).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The 2020 revision reframes the three-lines convention, retaining the distinction between operating-management roles (first line), risk-and-compliance oversight roles (second line), and internal audit (third line). The revision emphasises that the lines are roles rather than separate organisational units. For management response, the model clarifies that the first line owns the action plan and remediation execution, the second line provides challenge and oversight including review of the response before issuance, and the third line monitors implementation through the CAE follow-up process under IIA Standard 2500.A1.
- Link: https://www.theiia.org/en/content/position-papers/2020/the-iias-three-lines-model-an-update-of-the-three-lines-of-defense/ — IIA Three Lines Model (July 2020), section on the three roles
- Notes: The Three Lines Model is voluntary professional guidance from the IIA. It is widely adopted as the role taxonomy across US financial-services firms; specific firm implementations vary, particularly the placement of 1.5-line embedded business risk officers (treated in this skill as part of first-line operating management with a risk mandate).

## Cross-cutting overlay anchors

### 10. NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 — Cybersecurity examination findings

- Citation: New York Department of Financial Services, 23 NYCRR Part 500, "Cybersecurity Requirements for Financial Services Companies", §500.17(b) (annual CISO certification, Form B) and §500.04(b) (CISO report to senior governing body) (current text reflecting Second Amendment effective November 1, 2023, with phased compliance dates).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Part 500 examinations produce supervisory findings on the firm's cybersecurity program. Findings communicated by NYDFS may take the form of formal supervisory letters with Matters Requiring Attention-equivalent treatment. The cyber cross-cutting overlay covers the response conventions for Part 500-anchored findings, including the linkage to §500.17 cybersecurity-event notification (where the underlying event was also notified) and to §500.04 CISO oversight obligations.
- Link: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/cybersecurity — 23 NYCRR Part 500 §500.04 and §500.17 (verify current section labels post-Second-Amendment)
- Notes: Part 500 reach is to NYDFS-licensed covered entities; many cross-state and multinational firms are in scope through a NY licence even where their primary regulator is elsewhere. Verify section labels post-November-2023 amendments at write time.
