# Source anchors: attestation-pack

Cited regulatory sources this skill anchors to. Every primary anchor names the source, the section reference (or `[verify section]` flag), a one-paragraph paraphrase of what the source says that the skill relies on, and the link.

The skill cites these by file path. SKILL.md does not restate excerpts.

## Primary anchors

### 1. COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework (2013)

- Citation: Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission, "Internal Control — Integrated Framework" (May 2013), Components: Control Environment, Risk Assessment, Control Activities, Information and Communication, Monitoring Activities; seventeen Principles.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The 2013 framework sets out five components and seventeen principles for internal control over financial reporting and operations. Management uses the framework to assess design and operating effectiveness at the entity, division, operating unit, and function level. The attestation pack uses COSO components to organise the control inventory and the control-by-control attestation; SOX 404 management assessment is conventionally framed against COSO 2013.
- Link: https://www.coso.org/internal-control — Internal Control — Integrated Framework (2013), Principle 16 (ongoing and separate evaluations) and Principle 17 (deficiency communication)
- Notes: COSO is voluntary framework guidance, near-universally adopted as the SOX 404 framework in US public-company attestation. Distinct from COSO ERM 2017 which sits at the enterprise risk level.

### 2. Sarbanes-Oxley Sections 302 and 404 — sub-certification structures

- Citation: Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002, §302 (Corporate Responsibility for Financial Reports) and §404 (Management Assessment of Internal Controls); SEC implementing rules at 17 CFR §240.13a-14, §240.13a-15, §240.15d-14, §240.15d-15, and Item 308 of Regulation S-K.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §302 requires the principal executive officer and principal financial officer to certify quarterly and annual reports including responsibility for internal control over financial reporting and disclosure controls and procedures. §404 requires management's annual assessment of ICFR and (for accelerated filers) an external-auditor attestation. Process-owner sub-certifications are the firm's mechanism for laddering the assertions up to the executive certification; this skill produces the sub-certification, not the executive certification.
- Link: https://www.sec.gov/about/laws/soa2002.pdf — Sarbanes-Oxley Act (2002) §§302 and 404; https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240 — 17 CFR §240.13a-14, §240.13a-15, Item 308 of Regulation S-K
- Notes: Process-owner and function-head sub-certifications are firm-policy artifacts that ladder up to the executive certification. The executive certification itself is securities-counsel-led and out of scope for this skill.

### 3. PCAOB AS 2201 — Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting

- Citation: Public Company Accounting Oversight Board, Auditing Standard 2201, "An Audit of Internal Control Over Financial Reporting That Is Integrated with An Audit of Financial Statements" (formerly AS 5). PCAOB has periodically amended AS 2201; verify the operative text and any future-effective amendments at pack-write time, including the 2024 amendments to AS 1000 / general responsibilities and the AS 2201 follow-on amendments under PCAOB rulemaking [verify exact release numbers and effective dates against the operative PCAOB inspection-cycle calendar].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: AS 2201 sets the auditor's responsibilities for the integrated audit of ICFR and the financial statements. Sections govern management's responsibility (referenced via Item 308 of Reg S-K), control identification, evaluation of evidence, evaluation of identified deficiencies, and reporting. The attestation pack is structured to anticipate the AS 2201 evidence and deficiency lens because the external auditor's evaluation runs against the same control set the management assertion covers.
- Link: https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201 — PCAOB AS 2201
- Notes: PCAOB amendments are released under release numbers (e.g., 2024-005 for AS 1000 / general responsibilities; subsequent releases for AS 2201 follow-on amendments). Verify which amendments are operative for the audit-period under review and which are future-effective. The standard governs the auditor; management's assertion is structured to align with the evidence the auditor will test.

### 4. AICPA SSAE 18 — SOC 1 (AT-C 320) and SOC 2 (AT-C 105 / 205)

- Citation: American Institute of CPAs, Statement on Standards for Attestation Engagements No. 18, AT-C section 320 ("Reporting on an Examination of Controls at a Service Organization Relevant to User Entities' Internal Control Over Financial Reporting", SOC 1) and AT-C section 105 / 205 (general attestation requirements; SOC 2 examination of controls relevant to security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: SSAE 18 sets the management-assertion structure for SOC 1 (ICFR-relevant service-organisation controls) and SOC 2 (Trust Services Criteria). The user entity that consumes a service organisation's SOC report is responsible for identifying complementary user entity controls (CUECs), evaluating carve-outs, and providing CUEC-level evidence at the user entity. The attestation pack's evidence index includes both SOC report rows and CUEC-evidence rows where a SOC report is cited.
- Link: https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc — AICPA SSAE 18, AT-C section 320 (SOC 1) and AT-C section 105 / 205 (SOC 2); Trust Services Criteria
- Notes: SSAE 18 superseded SSAE 16 (effective May 2017). Trust Services Criteria are updated periodically by the AICPA Assurance Services Executive Committee; verify the current version at pack-write time.

### 5. BCBS 239 — Principle 1 (Governance) for risk-data attestation

- Citation: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, "Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting" (January 2013), Principle 1 (Governance) and supporting principles.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Principle 1 requires a bank's risk data aggregation capabilities and risk reporting practices to be subject to strong governance arrangements consistent with other principles and guidance established by the Basel Committee. The risk-data attestation use case (Head of Risk Reporting attesting to data quality and report integrity) anchors to Principle 1 for the governance assertion and to Principles 2-14 for the substantive control inventory.
- Link: https://www.bis.org/publ/bcbs239.htm — Principle 1 (Governance)
- Notes: BCBS 239 supervisory expectations have evolved through the BCBS Progress Reports (most recent thematic review applies; verify at pack-write time). The ECB Guide on effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting (May 2024 final) raises the SSM bar incrementally for SSM-supervised institutions.

### 6. U.S. interagency third-party risk management guidance (June 2023)

- Citation: Federal Reserve, FDIC, OCC, "Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management" (June 6, 2023), Section III.D (Ongoing Monitoring) and Section III.E (Termination).
- Excerpt-paraphrase: The 2023 guidance replaced the prior agency-specific guidance and sets a unified framework across planning, due diligence, contract negotiation, ongoing monitoring, and termination. Section III.D requires ongoing monitoring proportionate to the risk and criticality of the third-party relationship, including periodic reassessment of vendor risk, monitoring of contractual performance, and review of vendor financial condition. The vendor-management attestation use case anchors to this guidance.
- Link: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/06/09/2023-12340/interagency-guidance-on-third-party-relationships-risk-management — Section III.D (Ongoing monitoring) and Section III.E (Termination)
- Notes: The guidance applies to all banking organisations supervised by the agencies; community-bank-tailored examples are appended. State-chartered nonmember banks follow FDIC implementation; state-member banks follow FRB implementation; national banks and federal savings associations follow OCC implementation.

### 7. OCC Heightened Standards — 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D, board-level attestation linkage

- Citation: 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D, "OCC Guidelines Establishing Heightened Standards", Section IV (Roles and Responsibilities) [verify section labels at pack-write time].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Section IV sets the roles and responsibilities of the board, including the requirement that the board (or its risk committee) review and approve the Risk Governance Framework. Process-owner and function-head sub-certifications under the firm's certification framework typically ladder up to the board-level attestations the framework requires; the attestation pack supports that ladder.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-30/appendix-Appendix%20D%20to%20Part%2030 — 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D Section IV
- Notes: Heightened Standards apply to OCC-supervised insured national banks, federal savings associations, and federal branches above the asset threshold (currently $50 billion). Confirm section labels against current eCFR text at pack-write time.

## Sector-specific anchors (loaded by overlays)

### 8. NAIC Model Audit Rule — Model Regulation #205, ICFR for insurers

- Citation: NAIC Annual Financial Reporting Model Regulation (Model #205), Section 16 ("Management's Report of Internal Control over Financial Reporting") [verify section number against current NAIC text].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Model #205 Section 16 requires insurers above the premium threshold to file an annual management report of ICFR with their domiciliary state regulator. The management assertion structure parallels SOX §404 for public companies. Insurance overlay loads this anchor for insurer-attestation use cases.
- Link: https://content.naic.org/cipr-topics/audit-and-control — NAIC Model #205 Section 16 [verify section number]
- Notes: Adoption is state-by-state. Premium threshold is conventionally $500 million (single insurer) or $1 billion (insurance group) but varies by state; verify the carrier's domiciliary-state adoption at pack-write time.

### 9. SEC Rule 17a-5 — broker-dealer financial responsibility attestation

- Citation: SEC Rule 17a-5 (17 CFR §240.17a-5), "Reports to be made by certain brokers and dealers" [verify subsection at pack-write time].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Rule 17a-5 requires registered broker-dealers to file annual reports including a financial-responsibility report and (for carrying broker-dealers) compliance and exemption reports. The management assertions in those reports anchor to the firm's ICFR and to specific SEC financial-responsibility rules (Rule 15c3-1 net capital, Rule 15c3-3 customer-protection, segregation requirements). Capital-markets overlay loads this anchor for broker-dealer attestation use cases.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/subject-group-ECFR1c0e3a3a4eb2e1c/section-240.17a-5 — 17 CFR §240.17a-5 [verify subsection]
- Notes: PCAOB AS 3105 (audit of broker-dealer financial reports) governs the auditor work; this skill produces management's attestation, not the auditor's.

### 10. NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 §500.17(b) — annual notice of compliance (Form B)

- Citation: New York Department of Financial Services, 23 NYCRR Part 500 §500.17(b) "Notices to Superintendent — annual notice of compliance" (current text reflects the Second Amendment effective November 1, 2023, with phased compliance dates through November 2025) [verify section labels at pack-write time].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §500.17(b) requires covered entities to submit an annual written notice of compliance — Form B — signed by the highest-ranking executive and the CISO, certifying that the covered entity materially complied with the requirements of Part 500 for the prior calendar year, or a written acknowledgement (also signed by both) identifying the section(s) of Part 500 with which the covered entity has not complied, the nature and extent of non-compliance, and the remediation areas, timeline, and confirmation that the issue is or will be remediated. The cyber-annual attestation use case anchors here.
- Link: https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/cybersecurity — 23 NYCRR Part 500 §500.17(b)
- Notes: Form B is the named form. The signing-officer pair is the CISO plus the highest-ranking executive; the certification language and the acknowledgement-of-non-compliance template are NYDFS-prescribed. Verify the current Form B template at pack-write time.

### 11. GLBA Safeguards Rule §314.4(i) — annual report to board; §314.4(j) breach-notification trigger

- Citation: Federal Trade Commission, Standards for Safeguarding Customer Information, 16 CFR Part 314 (current text reflects December 2021 final rule with phased compliance through 2023, plus the November 13, 2023 amendment adding the notification trigger). §314.4(i) (annual written report to the board or governing body); §314.4(j) (notice to FTC of any unauthorised acquisition of unencrypted customer information of 500 or more consumers, no later than 30 days after discovery) [verify section labels — the December 2021 final rule reorganised §314.4 and earlier paraphrases that placed the annual-board-report at §314.4(g) reflect the pre-amendment numbering].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: §314.4(i) requires the qualified individual to report at least annually in writing to the board or equivalent governing body (or, where there is none, to a senior officer) on the firm's information-security program, covering overall program status, risk assessment, service-provider arrangements, testing results, security events, and recommendations. §314.4(j) (added November 2023) requires non-bank financial institutions in FTC scope to notify the FTC of breaches affecting 500 or more consumers within 30 days of discovery. Privacy overlay loads these anchors for nonbank financial institutions in scope of the Safeguards Rule.
- Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-314 — 16 CFR Part 314 §§314.4(i), 314.4(j)
- Notes: The December 2021 amendments restructured §314.4 (the older §314.4(g) annual-report cite is from the pre-amendment text). The November 2023 §314.4(j) breach-notification amendment is a separate trigger from the GLBA-bank parallel under the Interagency Guidance on Response Programs. Verify section labels at pack-write time.

## Cross-cutting overlays cited

### 12. NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (February 2024)

- Citation: National Institute of Standards and Technology, "Cybersecurity Framework 2.0" (February 26, 2024), Functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover.
- Excerpt-paraphrase: CSF 2.0 added the Govern function and reorganises the prior CSF 1.1 categories. Cyber-control attestations frequently use CSF 2.0 functions to organise the control inventory; cyber overlay loads this anchor for the framework framing.
- Link: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework — NIST CSF 2.0 (February 2024), Govern function section (GV)
- Notes: Voluntary. Frequently used as the firm's cyber-control framework framing for attestation purposes.

### 13. PCI DSS Attestation of Compliance (AOC)

- Citation: PCI Security Standards Council, PCI Data Security Standard v4.0 (March 2022; v4.0.1 June 2024), Attestation of Compliance (AOC) and Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ) forms [verify current version at pack-write time].
- Excerpt-paraphrase: Card-handling entities attest annually to PCI DSS compliance via the AOC (Level 1 merchants and service providers, validated by a QSA) or SAQ (lower-volume entities). The management assertion in the AOC structures to the twelve PCI DSS requirements. Payments-fintech overlay loads this anchor for entities in PCI scope.
- Link: https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library — PCI DSS v4.0 / v4.0.1 AOC, section on Attestation of Compliance forms [verify current version]
- Notes: PCI DSS v3.2.1 retired March 31, 2024; v4.0 in force with v4.0.1 minor revisions. Verify the current AOC form at pack-write time.
