# Cross-cutting overlay — conduct (UDAAP themes for deposit operations)

Default-on wherever the matrix touches consumer-facing decisions on fees, holds, disputes, account closure, or deposit-insurance representation. Conduct exposure on a deposit-operations program is the highest-frequency examiner-finding lane: the CFPB's Supervisory Highlights and Circular series and the prudential regulators' UDAP enforcement have run continuously on these themes through 2023-26.

The overlay carries the theme list to read into the relevant process-area rows. It does not substitute for the deeper review in `consumer-compliance-fair-lending/udaap-risk-review`; the matrix raises the flag and points to the deep-dive.

## Anchor frame

- **Dodd-Frank Act §§1031, 1036** — UDAAP authority for the CFPB.
- **FTC Act §5** — UDAP authority for the prudential regulators (OCC, FRB, FDIC) on the unfair-or-deceptive-acts-or-practices side.
- **CFPB Supervisory Highlights (current cycles)** — verify which issues are current and which themes are flagged on the analysis date.
- **CFPB Circular series** — verify which circulars are current. Circular 2022-06 on unanticipated overdraft fees [verify current state]; subsequent circulars on representment and surprise overdraft fees [verify].
- **FDIC misrepresentation rule, 12 CFR Part 328 Subpart B** — standards for non-bank entities (including fintech program operators) holding out deposit insurance and the bank's review obligation on customer-facing surfaces.
- **Reg E (12 CFR Part 1005)** §1005.6 unauthorised-transfer liability and §1005.11 dispute-timing — failures here are pursued as both Reg E findings and UDAAP findings.
- **Reg DD (12 CFR Part 1030)** — disclosure mechanics on fees; the conduct lane reads on top of the truth-in-savings disclosure regime.

## Theme list to read into the matrix

**Surprise overdraft fees and authorised-positive-settled-negative pattern.** A consumer authorises a transaction when the available balance is positive, but the transaction settles when the available balance is negative because of the order of operations on the ledger (intervening debit posts first). The consumer is charged an overdraft fee on a transaction the consumer reasonably believed would not trigger one. The CFPB has pursued this pattern as unfair under Dodd-Frank §1031(c). Read into the NSF, overdraft, and fee-disclosure process-area rows.

**Representment fees.** A returned-item is re-presented by the originator and triggers a second NSF or returned-item fee. The CFPB has pursued this pattern as unfair where the bank does not disclose and where the consumer cannot reasonably avoid the second fee. Read into the NSF, overdraft, and fee-disclosure process-area rows.

**Junk-fee themes.** Surprise fees, double fees, fees disclosed in fine print but applied broadly, fees not aligned with stated policy. Federal-regulator junk-fee enforcement has been a continuous theme through 2023-26; verify current cycle on the analysis date. Read into the NSF, overdraft, and fee-disclosure process-area rows and the periodic-statement aggregation row.

**Reg E §1005.11 dispute-timing failures presented as UDAAP.** The §1005.11 windows are 10 **business** days for the investigation, 45 **calendar** days extension with provisional credit, 90 **calendar** days extension for new accounts, point-of-sale transactions, and foreign-initiated transfers, and 3 **business** days post-investigation to notify the consumer of the results. Where any of these windows are missed, expect both Reg E findings and UDAAP findings. The provisional-credit duty under §1005.11(c) is the most-frequent breach. Read into every consumer-EFT process-area row carrying disputes.

**FDIC-insurance representation on fintech surfaces.** The Synapse-era enforcement and the **FDIC Part 328 final rule (December 2023) with effective-date extensions through 2024-2025** — verify current effective date for the engagement — require any "FDIC-insured" framing on fintech surfaces to be re-papered to the pass-through caveats. Marketing-claim review against 12 CFR Part 328 Subpart B sits in the advertising-review process area; the customer-facing-surface review sits in the FBO overlay; the periodic re-attestation sits in the exception-handling process area.

**Account closure and account hold without notice or recourse.** A recurring fintech-banking UDAAP theme that flows up to the sponsor bank. Control families: notice timing and content; customer-recourse channel; complaint-routing-to-sponsor-bank loop; account-closure root-cause review; hold-release SLA; redress posture for wrongful holds. Read into the FBO overlay, the funds-availability process-area rows, and the exception-handling process-area rows.

**Garnishment and federal-benefit-payment lookback errors.** Federal benefits not properly protected under 31 CFR Part 212; protected-amount calculation errors; notice-to-account-holder missing or untimely. Examiner attention on this theme is consistent. Read into the garnishments-and-levies process-area rows.

**Dormant-account reactivation without customer authorisation or with fee impact.** Reactivation that triggers fees or that occurs without the customer's clear request, especially where the account holder is elderly or vulnerable. Read into the escheatment-and-dormant-accounts process-area rows.

**Override-driven customer harm.** Exceptions and overrides applied at the deposit-operations level that result in customer-facing harm (a hold not released when it should be, a fee assessed when it should be waived, a dispute timed-out when it should have been escalated). The aggregate-exception report must include a UDAAP screen on overrides that affect customers. Read into the exception-handling process-area rows.

**Complaint-data triangulation.** CFPB Consumer Complaint Database vs internal complaint data vs fintech-program-routed complaint data. Material discrepancies are an examiner signal.

## What the overlay puts into the matrix

In the **NSF, overdraft, and fee-disclosure** process-area rows: the truth-in-savings disclosure mechanic is one lens; the UDAAP screen on the customer-impact side is the other. A row that reads only as a disclosure check misses the conduct lane. Pull the theme list above into the row's UDAAP-screen sub-column.

In the **consumer-EFT** process-area rows: the dispute-timing rows carry the UDAAP cross-walk (failure to meet the §1005.11 windows — 10 business days for the investigation, 45 / 90 calendar days for the relevant extensions, 3 business days post-investigation notification — is pursued both as Reg E and as UDAAP).

In the **funds-availability** process-area rows: the exception-hold notification row and the hold-release row carry the UDAAP cross-walk (delayed release without notice or recourse is pursued as UDAAP).

In the **FBO overlay**: the customer-facing deposit-insurance representation row carries the misrepresentation-rule cross-walk; the account-closure-and-hold row carries the UDAAP theme.

In the **exception-handling** process-area rows: the aggregate-exception report includes a UDAAP screen on overrides that affect customers; the periodic re-attestation on customer-facing deposit-insurance representation sits as an aggregate report.

In the **garnishments and levies** process-area rows: the federal-benefit-payment lookback control and the protected-amount-calculation control carry the UDAAP cross-walk.

## Common pitfalls (conduct lane)

- Reading the NSF and overdraft rows only as truth-in-savings disclosure checks. The conduct lane is where the recurring CFPB enforcement sits; read the rows through both lenses.
- Treating the FDIC misrepresentation rule as a marketing-only control. The bank's review obligation extends to the customer-facing surfaces of any sponsor-bank fintech program where the bank is named as the insured depository institution; the row sits in three places.
- Treating the dispute-timing breach rate as a Reg E metric only. The CFPB pursues breaches as both Reg E and UDAAP; the matrix must show the cross-walk.
- Skipping the aggregate-exception UDAAP screen. Override and exception logs are an examiner-favourite review point; aggregate reporting cadence and override-authority matrix do not substitute for a customer-impact screen.
- Treating dormant-account reactivation as a hygiene control. Vulnerable-customer protection is a recurring conduct theme.
