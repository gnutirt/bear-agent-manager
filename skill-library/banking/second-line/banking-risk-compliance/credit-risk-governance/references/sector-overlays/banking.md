# Banking sector overlay — credit-risk-governance

This skill is banking-sector-only by definition. The body of `SKILL.md` carries the workflow generically; this overlay carries the deeper banking-specific detail the body keeps off the surface: charter-by-charter rule cites, booklet edition tracking nuance, and the Heightened Standards cross-walk lines for credit at covered banks.

## CRE concentration screen — the framing the pack must keep

The 100% / 300% interagency CRE concentration screens are monitoring triggers, not regulatory caps. The 2006 Interagency CRE Concentration Guidance (71 FR 74580) explicitly frames them as triggers for enhanced risk-management expectations. Banks above either trigger are expected to apply heightened risk-management practices: portfolio stress-testing, robust risk-rating, granular concentration monitoring, more frequent board reporting on concentration position. They are not in violation. The 2015 Interagency Statement reaffirmed the framework and emphasised that banks above the 300% screen face heightened supervisory scrutiny, particularly in geographies or property types showing softening fundamentals.

The pack section 4 must phrase the position as a trigger for enhanced expectations, never as a regulatory cap. A pack that misframes the screen reads as not-ready and undermines the credibility of the rest of the credit-governance review.

## Heightened Standards cross-walk for credit (covered banks)

For covered banks under 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D ($50B+ avg total consolidated assets), section 8 of the pack runs a cross-walk between the Appendix D risk-governance-framework expectations and the credit-risk substance from sections 1 through 7. The cross-walk lines:

| Appendix D element [verify section labels] | Credit-risk evidence the pack carries forward |
|---|---|
| §III risk-governance framework — independence of the second line | Section 7 evidence: credit-review function reporting line, scope authority, sample-based challenge results |
| §III risk-governance framework — risk-appetite statement | Section 1 + section 4 evidence: credit-policy framework alignment, concentration thresholds expressed as risk-appetite metrics |
| §III risk-governance framework — three-lines-of-defense maturity | Section 7 + section 8 evidence: 1L origination, 1.5L portfolio management, 2L credit risk and second-line credit-review, 3L internal audit coverage of credit |
| §IV board's role | Section 4 + section 5 + section 6 evidence: board credit-committee charter and minutes, ALLL / ACL methodology approval, prior-board-approval gate for Reg O insider credit |
| Rebuttable-presumption posture (where applied) | Tail evidence: where the OCC has applied the rebuttable presumption, the pack carries the bank's response under §V [verify section label] |

For banks approaching the $50B threshold (typically $40B+), run the section 8 cross-walk forward-looking; OCC examiners will probe in advance of the trigger.

## Asset-quality CAMELS-component handoff

The credit-governance pack feeds the asset-quality CAMELS-component preparation in the broader supervision-readiness pack. The asset-quality section in `banking-supervision-readiness` reads from this pack: classified-asset trend, ALLL / ACL methodology summary, concentration position, and the open-issue rows on credit. The credit-governance pack stays the deeper artifact; the supervision-readiness pack consumes the headline.

Where the cycle is a topical credit-administration examination (OCC asset-quality scope, FDIC asset-quality and management scope, FRB BHC consolidated supervision focusing on credit), this pack is the primary deliverable and the supervision-readiness pack reduces to a thin wrapper around the credit-governance substance.

## State-banking-department interaction

State-chartered banks (state-member and state non-member, plus state savings associations) carry a state banking department as a co-regulator alongside the FRB or FDIC. State examination cycles often run jointly with the federal cycle. Where the state banking department applies a state-specific credit-administration regulation (single-borrower lending limit at the state level, state-level concentration guidance), cite the state regulation in addition to the federal anchor. The pack carries the state-specific cite as a separate row in the source trace; do not assume the federal cite covers state-specific expectations.

NYDFS for NY-licensed banks is the most-cited example; other state banking departments apply their own credit-administration regulations and the pack must consult the operative state-level rules at write time.
