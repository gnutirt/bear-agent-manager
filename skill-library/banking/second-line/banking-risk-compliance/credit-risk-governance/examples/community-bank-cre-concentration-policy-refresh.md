Public-source-derived. No named institution. Roles only.

## Engagement context

A community bank (asset size band <10B, approximately $6B in total consolidated assets, FDIC-regulated state non-member bank) is refreshing its CRE concentration governance after the loan portfolio passed the 300% of total capital interagency CRE concentration screen. The credit risk committee has commissioned a second-line review of the proposed updated CRE policy. Source posture is public-plus-firm-policy; the access population is the credit risk committee, the chief credit officer, and the head of credit risk review.

## Executive summary

- Proposed CRE policy refresh covers most of the supervisory expectations on concentration governance, with one material gap on stress-testing methodology.
- Current CRE position is 320% of total capital, above the 300% interagency screen and inside the 350% internal limit the proposed policy would set.
- Second-line recommendation is to require enhanced board reporting at every 25-basis-point step above 300% rather than a single trip-wire at the proposed 350% internal limit; the trip-wire framing under-uses the supervisory framing of the screen as a graduated-expectation trigger.
- ACL / CECL methodology uses a vintage-cohort approach for CRE; reviewer question on whether the qualitative-factor framework captures the post-300% concentration risk premium consistently with the policy refresh.
- Reg O / Reg W not material this cycle; quarterly Reg O reporting is current.
- Overall confidence medium; high on policy-alignment scope, medium on concentration governance, medium on allowance methodology.

## Section 1: Credit policy alignment

The proposed CRE policy refresh covers six of the seven coverage elements in the cross-walk against the Comptroller's Handbook Concentrations of Credit framework (current edition) [verify edition]. The seventh (stress-testing) is referenced but the policy does not specify methodology, frequency, or board-reporting cadence. Public criteria: Comptroller's Handbook Concentrations of Credit booklet [verify edition]; Interagency Guidance on Concentrations in CRE Lending (December 12, 2006); 2015 Interagency Statement.

Gap: stress-testing methodology, frequency, and board-reporting cadence not specified.

Second-line recommendation: chief credit officer to specify the stress-testing methodology in the policy (scenario set, tail severity, frequency, board-reporting form) before policy ratification.

## Section 2: Underwriting framework

CRE-specific underwriting standards updated for stabilised-vs-construction. Trailing-12-month exception rate sits in the 5-10% band. One open observation: exception aggregate is reported quarterly without trend commentary, against the supervisory expectation that aggregate-tracking includes trend analysis. Reviewer question on whether the policy refresh should embed a monthly trend commentary requirement.

## Section 3: Risk-rating discipline

CRE-specific rating drivers documented. Reviewer question on whether the granular pass-grade distribution (currently four pass grades) is sufficient to support the ACL segmentation approach in section 5.

## Section 4: Concentration governance

Current CRE position is 320% of total capital, above the 300% interagency screen for total CRE under the December 2006 Interagency Guidance. The construction-and-development position sits below the 100% screen. The proposed policy raises the internal limit from the current 300% to 350%.

Second-line recommendation: enhanced board reporting at every 25-basis-point step above 300% (i.e., 300%, 325%, 350%, 375%) rather than a single trip-wire at 350%. The trip-wire framing under-uses the supervisory framing of the 300% screen as a graduated-expectation trigger. Cite Interagency Guidance on Concentrations in CRE Lending (December 12, 2006) and the 2015 Interagency Statement.

## Section 5: Allowance methodology oversight

CECL methodology uses a vintage-cohort approach for CRE. Model-validation status: validated within the last 12 months by model risk; no open findings. Qualitative-factor framework approved by the ALLL / ACL governance committee, with seven qualitative factors covering economic conditions, lending policy and procedures, collection and other policies, experience of credit-administration staff, concentration, external factors, and other.

Reviewer question on whether the qualitative-factor framework captures the post-300% concentration risk premium consistently with the policy refresh. Recommend the ALLL / ACL governance committee refresh the qualitative-factor framework alignment with the concentration policy at the next quarterly meeting.

## Section 6: Reg O and Reg W applicability

Not material this cycle. No insider-CRE transactions in the trailing 12 months. Quarterly Reg O Report (§215.9) filed on time; insider-list refresh confirmed within the last 30 days. Reg W not applicable; affiliate inventory current.

## Section 7: Second-line challenge of first-line lending decisions

Sample-based challenge of 30 CRE files in the trailing quarter: concurrence rate in the 75-90% band; override rate <5%. Findings summary: documentation light on construction-loan inspection cadence in 4 of 30 files. Action item for first-line: refresh the construction-loan inspection-cadence checklist. Independence of the credit-review function: reports to chief risk officer, with a board-approved charter.

## Section 8: Heightened Standards posture

Not applicable; asset size $6B is below the $50B threshold under 12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D.
