# Source anchors — bank-fintech-partnership-review

Named, dated public regulatory sources this skill anchors to for the bank-side principal-supervisory partnership pack on a US bank-fintech relationship. ## Interagency third-party risk lifecycle

- **Interagency Guidance on Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management** — joint final guidance issued by the OCC, FRB, and FDIC, June 6, 2023; OCC Bulletin 2023-17 / FRB SR 23-4 / FDIC FIL-29-2023. The published guidance is organised by lifecycle stage rather than numbered sections; cite by lifecycle phase: planning, due diligence and third-party selection, contract negotiation, ongoing monitoring, termination, plus governance (oversight and accountability, independent reviews, documentation and reporting). Risk-based tailoring is the through-line; criticality classification (critical activity vs lower-risk) drives diligence, contracting, and monitoring intensity. https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2304.htm and https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2023/bulletin-2023-17.html and https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2023/fil23029.html.
- **OCC, FRB, FDIC Joint Statement on Banks' Arrangements with Third Parties to Deliver Bank Deposit Products and Services** — July 25, 2024 [verify section labels and exact title]. Highlights deposit-program risks: end-user reconciliation, ledgering, FBO-account governance, beneficial-ownership clarity for the recordkeeping-bank relationship, contractual flow-down of bank obligations. https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20240725a.htm [verify URL].
- **OCC Bank-Fintech Request for Information** — published July 2024 with subsequent OCC commentary [verify final form and any subsequent bulletin]. Signals OCC examiner expectations on BaaS-program governance, sponsor-bank capacity, end-user reconciliation, and middleware-platform risk.
- **OCC Bulletin on Bank-Fintech Arrangements** [verify bulletin number and effective date — OCC has issued multiple BaaS-relevant bulletins post-2023]. https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/.

## Examination authority over bank service providers

- **Bank Service Company Act, 12 USC §1867** — examination, regulation, and notification framework for bank service providers. **§1867(c)** is the bank's obligation to notify its primary federal regulator within 30 days of entering into a contract for, or starting to receive, a covered service from a bank service provider. **§1867(b)** and (c) together give the federal banking agencies authority to regulate and examine the performance of those services to the same extent as if performed by the bank on its own premises (the contracted-services examination authority that flows through the bank to the provider). The contract-level flow-down — examination access, books-and-records access, regulator-cooperation language — is the load-bearing clause set in the partnership pack; without it, the regulator can still examine the provider, but the bank carries operational and contractual risk on access and on its own §1867(c) notification posture. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/1867.
- **OCC Comptroller's Handbook — Third-Party Relationships: Risk Management booklet** (current edition) [verify section and current edition post-2023 guidance]. OCC examiner-facing detail on diligence, contract, and monitoring expectations as applied by OCC supervisors. https://www.occ.gov/publications-and-resources/publications/comptrollers-handbook/index-comptrollers-handbook.html.

## Reg O (insider transactions) — 12 CFR Part 215

- **Regulation O — 12 CFR Part 215, Loans to Executive Officers, Directors, and Principal Shareholders of Member Banks**. Sections used in the partnership review: §215.2 (definitions, including executive officer, director, principal shareholder, related interest), §215.3 (extension of credit definition), §215.4 (general prohibitions, terms and creditworthiness), §215.5 (additional restrictions on loans to executive officers), §215.7 (extensions of credit outstanding on the date a person becomes an insider), §215.8 (records of member banks), §215.9 (reports by executive officers). The screen runs at every BaaS or sponsor-program partnership intake and on refresh: identify whether any fintech principal, executive officer, or related interest is an insider of the partner bank or its affiliates. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-215.

## Reg W (transactions with affiliates) — 12 CFR Part 223

- **Regulation W — 12 CFR Part 223, Transactions Between Member Banks and Their Affiliates**. The skill draws on Subpart A (§§223.1-223.16: affiliate definition including the 25%-control / management-interlock tests, the 10% per-affiliate and 20% aggregate quantitative limits on covered transactions, the §223.13 collateral requirements, the §223.14 low-quality-asset prohibition, and the §223.15 / §223.16 exemptions — including the sister-bank exemption at §223.41(a) [verify exact paragraph]) and Subpart F (§§223.51-223.56: market-terms requirement for §23B transactions). The screen determines whether the fintech is an affiliate (common control through a holding-company structure, management interlock, or 25% ownership) and, if so, whether the contemplated covered transactions sit inside the §§223.11 / 223.12 limits with §223.13 collateral. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-II/subchapter-A/part-223.

## Reg E (consumer EFT) — 12 CFR Part 1005

- **Regulation E — 12 CFR Part 1005, Electronic Fund Transfer Act implementation**. Sections used: §1005.2(i) (financial-institution definition — the entity that holds the consumer's account or issues the access device; in a sponsor-bank deposit program the bank typically holds the account and is the financial institution), §1005.6 (consumer liability for unauthorised EFTs and the timing of the consumer's notice), §1005.7 (initial disclosures of EFT terms), §1005.8 (change-in-terms notice), §1005.10 (preauthorised transfers), §1005.11 (error resolution: investigation within 10 business days, with provisional credit if the institution extends to the 45-calendar-day full-investigation window — 90 calendar days for new accounts, point-of-sale debit-card transactions, and foreign-initiated transfers; written notification to the consumer of the results within three business days after completing the investigation), §1005.13 (administrative enforcement and recordkeeping), §1005.18 (prepaid accounts subpart, where the program is prepaid). The bank as financial institution bears the §1005.11 timing obligation regardless of which entity does the operational intake; the partnership review tests the bank's fallback when the fintech misses an intake event. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1005.

## Reg DD (truth in savings) — 12 CFR Part 1030

- **Regulation DD — 12 CFR Part 1030, Truth in Savings**. Sections used: §1030.3 (general disclosure requirements; clear-and-conspicuous standard), §1030.4 (account disclosures: APY, interest rate, balance methods, fees, transaction limits, minimum balance), §1030.5 (subsequent disclosures: change-in-terms 30-day notice; maturity-related disclosures for time accounts), §1030.8 (advertising rules: APY accuracy, no-misleading-advertising standard, the seven triggering terms), §1030.9 (enforcement and recordkeeping). The bank as depository institution bears §1030.4 disclosure responsibility and §1030.8 advertising-rule risk on fintech-shipped marketing copy in the bank's name. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1030.

- **12 CFR Part 30 Appendix D — OCC Heightened Standards for large banks**. Sections include II (covered bank scope), III (risk-governance framework, including third-party-risk integration), IV (board's role) [verify section labels — published Appendix D uses letters without the III.B-style sub-numbering used colloquially]. ## Pass-through deposit insurance recordkeeping

- **12 CFR Part 330 — FDIC Deposit Insurance Coverage**. Sections used for sponsor-bank deposit programs: §330.5 (recordkeeping for deposits held in custodial accounts; requirements for the bank to identify the principal owners) [verify section]; the FBO-account contractual flow-down to the fintech (and any middleware platform) is the operational seam where the bank's recordkeeping obligation depends on the fintech's ledger reconciliation. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-330.

## BSA / AML for sponsor-bank programs

- **FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual** (current edition) [verify section]. The inter-agency manual that drives BSA / AML examination procedures applied to the bank's responsibility for the sponsor-program end-user customer base. https://bsaaml.ffiec.gov/manual.
- **31 CFR §1020.220 — Customer Identification Program rule for banks**. Bank's CIP responsibility for customers of record; the program may rely on a third party for CIP performance under §1020.220(a)(6) but the bank retains responsibility. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1020/subpart-B/section-1020.220.
- **31 CFR §1010.230 — Beneficial-ownership rule for legal-entity customers** (the bank-facing CDD rule on legal-entity customers; in force and operative for sponsor-bank programs at intake and on triggering events). This is distinct from the FinCEN beneficial-ownership reporting rule under the Corporate Transparency Act (§1010.380), which is a reporting-company-facing rule whose docket has been active across 2024-2025 and whose current state should be verified separately. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.230.

## Cross-cutting overlays — anchors

### Cyber

- **23 NYCRR Part 500 — NYDFS Cybersecurity Requirements for Financial Services Companies** (as amended November 1, 2023). Sections used: §500.6 (audit trail), §500.9 (risk assessment), §500.11 (third-party service-provider security policy and contractual protections including notification, due diligence, and minimum cybersecurity practices), §500.16 (incident-response and BCDR plan), §500.17(a) (notice to superintendent within 72 hours of determining a cybersecurity event meets the rule's criteria). For state-chartered partner banks supervised by NYDFS and for fintechs operating in the NY footprint. https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/cybersecurity.
- **FFIEC IT Examination Handbook — Information Security and Outsourcing Technology Services booklets** (current editions) [verify edition and section]. The technology-control expectations layered onto fintech relationships that are also ICT service providers. https://ithandbook.ffiec.gov/.
- **GLBA Safeguards Rule — 16 CFR Part 314, especially §314.4** (administrative, technical, and physical safeguards; includes the December 2021 amendments) [verify section labels for bank-regulator parallels]. Applies through the bank's affiliate scope; the fintech's safeguards posture is contractually flowed down. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-314.
- **SEC Cybersecurity Risk Management, Strategy, Governance, and Incident Disclosure rule** (effective December 18, 2023) — Item 1.05 of Form 8-K (material cybersecurity incident disclosure within four business days of materiality determination) and Item 106 of Reg S-K (annual cyber risk-management and governance disclosure). For the partner bank's BHC where it is a public registrant; a cyber event at the fintech that materially affects the bank can roll into a BHC disclosure obligation. https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2023/33-11216.pdf.
- **12 CFR Part 53 (OCC) / Part 225 (FRB) / Part 304 (FDIC) — Computer-Security Incident Notification rule** [verify subsection]. 36-hour notification to primary federal regulator for "notification incidents" at the bank or at a bank service provider that cause or are likely to cause material disruption. The fintech as a bank service provider triggers the rule at the bank's level. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-I/part-53.
